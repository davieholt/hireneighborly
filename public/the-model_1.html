The model

Where the money comes from,
and where it goes.

Neighbors never pay. You never pay. Businesses owned by people who live there pay to be seen first, and you keep a quarter of everything your neighborhood earns.

Five rungs, and the bottom one is free

Everything below is per year. One invoice, no monthly billing, and every renewal lands on the same date the calendar goes to print.

Calendar Sponsor$5,000 /year

The refrigerator, plus the top of the site, plus their category.

  • One of four spots on the school calendar magnet, beside the partner
  • Mailed to every household, not just the ones who go looking
  • On the fridge from the first day of school to the last

How a category stacks. Calendar Sponsor if there is one, then First Call, then the two Short List spots, then Featured, then everyone else free. A Calendar Sponsor takes the top of their own category automatically, which means First Call in that category is not for sale, but Short List still is. No category is ever fully closed.

Four calendar spots is a hard cap, forever. A magnet with fifteen businesses on it is too big and ends up in a drawer. Four is the number that keeps it on the refrigerator, which is the entire point. It also makes those four the only thing here that genuinely cannot be bought any other way.

Smaller neighborhoods earn less, honestly. At roughly 1,300 homes it is closer to $25,750 and a $6,438 share. At 600 homes, around $9,350 and $2,338. The calendar is always four spots no matter the size, so the smaller the neighborhood, the bigger a share of the whole thing those four conversations become.

Paid monthly, automatically, from the first dollar. A business checks out online, the money lands with us, your share pays out every month. You never send an invoice, never chase a check, and never front a dollar for printing or postage.

Why the split is what it is

You do
  • Know people, and keep knowing them
  • Ask businesses to join
  • Sell four calendar spots a year
  • Show up to the partner call once a month
We do
  • Build and host the page
  • Design, print and mail the magnet
  • Checkout, invoices, receipts, renewals
  • The residency check, automated
  • Quarterly reports to every paying business
  • Support, content and the software

That job description has a name and a market rate. It is a manufacturer's rep, and reps take 15% to 30% for exactly this trade: you bring the relationships, we bring the product, the platform and the fulfillment. 25% sits in the middle of a well established band.

So the pitch is not "make money." $14,400 is real, but if that is the only reason you are here it will not hold. The reason to do this is that you become the person every business owner in your neighborhood knows, and your name is on 2,200 refrigerators for a school year. The share is on top of that.

Which is why this fits an agent, a contractor, an insurance agent or an advisor, and does not fit somebody looking for income.
