How this started

A plumber came out to our house last year. Somewhere between the sink and the driveway he mentioned he lived four streets over. We had been calling strangers for six years.

Rebecca started a list. She called neighbors one at a time and asked who they were and what they did. Sixty-four businesses later, every one of them owned by somebody who lives in Lakewood, the list turned out to be the thing everybody wanted and nobody had.

That is the whole idea. It is a list of neighbors who do work, and it only works because a neighbor made it.

Three things this is not

Not this

A review site

No stars, no rankings, no reviews. Keep hiring the way you always have, and ask your neighbors.

Not this

A lead broker

We never take a cut of anybody's job. A phone number is a phone number.

Not this

A pay to play list

Being listed is free forever. Most of the list is free on purpose. Paying only moves where a name sits.

Who runs one

One person per neighborhood, and they have to live there. The job is relationships, not software. Everything else is ours.

Usually

A real estate agent

They already know everybody, and they know who just moved in.

Often

A contractor or an agent

Insurance, lending, home services. Somebody whose day job runs on being known.

Sometimes

The person who runs the group

Whoever already answers the questions in the neighborhood Facebook group.

What we believe

Neighbors first

The list is free for the people who live there, permanently. There is no version of this where a neighbor pays.

Living here is the whole bar

We do not rate anybody. We confirm they live where they say they live, once a year.

Money should be visible

Paid spots are labeled. Nobody has to guess why a name is at the top.

Small on purpose

One neighborhood at a time, one person running each. We would rather have ten that work than a hundred that do not.
