How this started

A plumber came out to our house last year. Somewhere between the sink and the invoice we ended up standing in the living room laughing, because it turned out his daughter used to babysit our kids.

Paying that bill felt different. Not cheaper. Different. The trust was already there before he ever walked in the door.

Not everybody has a neighbor for every job. But the neighbors are already here. Nobody had ever made the list.

So Rebecca started asking. In the neighborhood Facebook group, at the pool, in the school pickup line. Sixty-four businesses turned up, every one of them owned by somebody who lives in Lakewood. A painter. A plumber. A photographer. A woman who roasts coffee.

She called the property owners association to make sure we were doing it the right way. Then she made a handful of phone calls to businesses who wanted to be seen first, and raised ten thousand dollars in a neighborhood of 2,200 homes, enough to put a magnet on every refrigerator in it.

Rebecca is not a publisher and I am not a marketer. She just knew everybody, and I knew how to build the thing. That is the whole job, and if you are the person your neighborhood already asks, we can do the other half for you.

Lakewood Neighbors is run by Rebecca Green, a REALTOR® with Real Broker LLC who has lived in Lakewood for years, and Davie Holt, who builds the rest of it. We are partners, and we built the first one in our own neighborhood before we offered it to anybody else's.

How it works

Five steps. Two of them are yours.

1

You apply, and we talk

A real conversation with both of us about your neighborhood, who you already know in it, and whether this is a fit. We take a small number of neighborhoods a year and we say no more often than yes.

2

We build your page

The page, the join form and the residency check, on your own web address or ours. Usually about a week, and you do not touch any of it.

3

You point neighbors at it

Post it in the group. Mention it at the pool. Businesses fill out the join form themselves, we confirm they live there, and they go on the list free.

4

The ones who want to be seen first pay for it

Four presenting spots at the top. One exclusive seat per trade. You have those conversations, because you are the one they trust. We handle the checkout, the invoices, the receipts and the renewals. You never send a bill.

5

Everybody gets in a room

The businesses holding a seat form your neighborhood's Council. They meet, they send each other work, and once a year they decide together who keeps a seat.

The Council is the review system

We are never going to do star ratings. Anonymous strangers grading your neighbors is the opposite of the point.

So instead, one business per trade holds a seat. They meet. They send each other work. And once a year the people in that room decide together whether a seat gets renewed.

Nobody is ever removed from the list. They just do not keep the seat.

What we believe

Six things. We will not quietly stop meaning any of them once this gets bigger.

A neighbor is a better filter than an algorithm

You are going to see this person at the mailbox. That is a stronger guarantee than five stars from somebody you will never meet.

Nobody buys their way onto the list

Being listed is free and it always will be. A list that is not complete is not useful to anybody, and it stops being true the moment it has a price.

We do not put numbers on people

No stars, no rankings, no grades. Your neighbors already talk to each other. We are not going to reduce someone's livelihood to a score.

The person running it has to live there

Not a franchisee. Not a call center in another state. Somebody whose kids go to the same schools and who has to face everyone at the pool.

Money should be visible

When a business pays for a spot, the page says so. You should always be able to tell who paid and who did not. That includes the person running the page.

We would rather be small and true

We are not trying to cover the country. We are trying to be right about one neighborhood, and then be right about the next one.

Three things this is not

Better you hear it now than in month four.

× It is not a lead marketplace. Nobody is bidding on you. Nobody is getting sold your phone number. There is no app and there never will be.
× It is not a paycheck replacement. Keep your job, that is the point. The money is real but it is a share, not a salary. What makes this worth doing is being the person every business owner in your neighborhood knows, and that pays you somewhere else.
× It is not fast. Year one you build the list and cover your costs. Year two is when it pays, because everything is already built and the relationships already exist.

How we choose

One partner per neighborhood, and we do not take every neighborhood. Your name ends up next to ours on something 2,200 households look at, so we would rather go slowly and pick well.
